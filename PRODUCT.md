# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences that must both feel at home on the same site:

**Audience B — B2B clients and partners (primary for this website):** Mid-sized companies needing custom software development, startups seeking a technical partner with real product vision, regional tech ecosystem investors. Profile: Andrés, 38, operations manager in San José, tired of overpriced consultancies that deliver late. Wants a partner who speaks with technical authority and ships real product. Expects Apple-level quality at startup speed.

**Audience A — End users of D8's own products (secondary):** Condo administrators, independent barbers, SMEs with tax obligations, public transit operators. Profile: María, 45, condo admin in Escazú, running everything on WhatsApp and Excel. Wants professional, reliable, requires no training. She doesn't care who built it — she cares that it works and looks trustworthy.

The brand must communicate with equal weight to both: accessible to the end user, technically rigorous for the corporate client and investor.

## Product Purpose

D8 is a product engineering firm that scans industries with fragmented or outdated solutions, redesigns what exists, and builds what's missing — from mechatronic systems to digital platforms — operating without third-party dependency for the technology core. It doesn't replicate, it synthesizes. It doesn't patch, it rebuilds with engineering judgment where others left gaps unresolved.

D8 also operates as a "branded house": the umbrella brand for its own products (MiCondos, MiBarbero, and future products) plus services for third-party clients. Everything that ships from D8 — own products or client work — carries a recognizable quality seal. Like "Designed by Apple in California" but from the Costa Rican tech ecosystem.

Success for this website: a potential client or partner lands on the home page and immediately feels they are dealing with a technically impeccable, aspirational but reachable company — without needing to read much copy. The logo and the craft do the talking.

## Brand Personality

Futurista · Inteligente · Confiable · Limpia · Poderosa

Voice: direct, confident, spare. No fluff. Engineering judgment expressed through restraint, not decoration.

Tone goal: "Aspirational without being unattainable. Premium without being exclusive. Technically impeccable without needing to explain it." Next-gen without shouting.

The name origin carries the philosophy: D8 = Day 8. After the biblical seven days of creation, Day 8 is the day of human building on what was created. The next iteration, built on what already works. Not reinventing — synthesizing and advancing.

## Anti-references

**Visual anti-patterns (hard no):**
- Generic engineering clichés: gears, wrenches, circuit boards as decoration
- Generic bar charts as visual elements
- Startup palette: neon green, saturated yellows, colors that "scream"
- Mascots or character illustrations
- Retro, vintage, hand-drawn, or childish styles
- 80s gradients, vaporwave, glitch effects — anything gimmick-dependent that will age badly
- Purple-to-blue gradients (specifically called out)
- Bounce animations
- Generic SaaS hero gradients
- Cards inside cards
- Stock photo heroes
- Inter font (use Space Grotesk)
- Taglines embedded in the logo, founding year callouts, "since 2025"

**Brand anti-references:** Any Costa Rican tech company website that looks like a WordPress template. Any offshore dev agency that looks interchangeable with ten others.

## Design Principles

1. **The seal speaks for itself.** Every page, component, and line of copy should be good enough that "D8 made this" is a guarantee, not a question. Craft is not decoration — it is the product.

2. **Simple outside, powerful inside.** The Apple/Linear/Vercel reference family: restraint on the surface, density of capability underneath. No feature parades. No capability laundry lists. Show through quality, not through enumeration.

3. **Tech hints are discovered, not announced.** Subtle technical references (a glow, a geometric detail, a precise grid) exist in the design but don't announce themselves. The user notices them on second look. Nothing gimmicky; everything earned.

4. **The identity must be relevant in 2030.** Zero trend dependency. Geometric minimalism with high containment. Nothing that requires a trend to work. Whitespace is an element, not a gap.

5. **Dual-audience fluency.** The site must read as professional and trustworthy to a 45-year-old condo admin AND as technically rigorous to a 38-year-old CTO. The language, hierarchy, and tone must serve both without condescending to either.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum.
- Sufficient contrast on dark backgrounds (d8 purple #9900ff on dark — verify contrast ratios carefully; pure purple on dark can fail AA).
- Keyboard navigable. Screen reader compatible with semantic HTML.
- Reduced motion: honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for all Framer Motion animations.
- Body text line length capped at 65–75ch.
